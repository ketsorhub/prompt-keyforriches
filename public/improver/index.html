    How to write a good AI prompt
    

    A prompt is simply the instruction you give an AI. The clearer the instruction, the better the answer. Most weak prompts fail for the same few reasons, and each one is easy to fix.
    

    1. Give the AI a role
    

    Tell the AI who it should be, for example “You are an experienced travel writer.” A role sets the tone, the vocabulary and the level of detail, so the answer sounds like an expert instead of a generic reply.
    

    2. Add context
    

    Explain the situation: who the answer is for, what you already know and why you need it. A short paragraph of background changes a vague answer into one that fits your real situation.
    

    3. State one clear task
    

    Begin with a strong action word such as write, compare, summarise or plan. One task per prompt works best. If you need several things, ask for them in separate steps.
    

    4. Say what format you want
    

    Do you want bullet points, a table, a short email or three headlines? If you do not say, the AI will guess. Naming the format and the length, for example “under 200 words”, saves you rewriting.
    

    5. Set limits
    

    Tell the AI what to avoid: no jargon, no made-up facts, no more than five items. Limits keep the answer focused and easier to trust.
    

    6. Show an example
    

    If you have a sample of the style you like, paste it in and say “write in this style”. One good example is often worth a whole paragraph of description.
    

    7. Ask the AI to check itself
    

    Finish with a request such as “If anything is unclear, ask me questions first.” This stops the AI from guessing and usually gives a better first answer.
